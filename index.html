Why anyone pays $99 for ten minutes

Every exchange, broker and payout processor has the same silent killer: a wrong destination address. One mistyped prefix on a chain with no recovery and the money is gone — no chargeback, no support ticket that helps.

Before a partner approves a new payout corridor, they need a clean corpus: addresses per chain, correctly formatted, labelled by wallet source, in volumes no single engineer can paste by hand. We assemble that corpus, our partners pay per accepted record, and you keep the lion's share of the fee.

Same reason a hardware vendor pays for a QA sweep — sampling is cheap, one bad unit shipped is not.

Questions

Do you get access to my wallet?

No. Only the public address string is used. Spending keys never leave your machine, and the checker only reads public chain data.

Why do you pay for a check?

Address verification is cheap to run and the queue data is useful to the networks we partner with. You get a slice of that.

When does the bonus arrive?

Immediately after verification, in the same coin as the address. Minimum cashout is one cent, so a single check is already withdrawable.

Is $99 per set real or a headline?

It is the top tier: 200 addresses with wallet-source labels, which really does take about ten minutes at a normal typing pace. Small sets pay small amounts — 5c for one. Nothing pays $99 for a single paste.

What gets a set rejected?

Fabricated addresses, duplicates inside the same set, or labels that plainly contradict the chain. Rejected sets can be corrected and resubmitted; there is no penalty.

Is the download required?

No. The browser check works on its own; the app only adds offline checking and payment notifications.
